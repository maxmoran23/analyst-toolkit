## Introduction

This memo records the investigation of Halcyon Drywall & Interiors LLC ("Halcyon"), a fictional residential interior-finishing contractor, and Devorah Tannehill, its sole member-manager and the authorized signer on all three accounts in scope. The accounts are a business checking account (****3318), a business savings account (****3326), and Tannehill's personal checking account (****0471) — all held at the same institution and all controlled by the same person, which is why they are described in this memo as "linked." The period covered is 2026-01-06 to 2026-04-24.

The activity is notable because, over those sixteen weeks, the three linked accounts were used to receive funds into one account and then move them in rapid, near-equal portions through the other two before sending them out again — a circular and self-cancelling pattern of internal transfers and onward payments that does not correspond to the ordinary cash flow of a contracting business. The investigation was opened after a transaction-monitoring alert flagged the rapid-movement pattern; this memo presents what the investigation established about how the funds moved, in time order. The term used throughout for this pattern is **layering** — the movement of funds through a sequence of accounts and transactions in a way that adds steps and obscures the connection between where the money entered and where it left.

## Conclusion

The investigation determined that, between 2026-01-06 and 2026-04-24, three accounts under the common control of one signer were used in a recurring monthly pattern: a sizeable inbound credit to the business checking account, immediately fragmented into rapid internal transfers across all three accounts, partly cycled back, and then sent out in recurring payments — $441,000.00 to one external counterparty for which no supporting trade documentation was produced, and $318,000.00 to accounts in the signer's own name at other institutions. The internal movement of $1,310,000.00 across 22 transfers exceeds the $882,000.00 actually received, confirming that the same funds were moved repeatedly between the linked accounts.

This pattern of layered, self-cycling movement through commonly controlled accounts is not consistent with the ordinary cash flow of a contracting business, and the explanation given by the customer — cash-flow management and payments to a materials supplier — was not corroborated by invoices, contracts, or delivery records when those were requested. On the evidence gathered, the business purpose of the activity is **unexplained**. The investigation does not conclude that any specific offense occurred; it concludes that the activity is unexplained and that the layering pattern, the unverified onward payments, and the absence of supporting documentation together warrant a suspicious-activity-report referral. The account relationship is recommended for referral to the function that prepares and decides on regulatory filings, and for enhanced monitoring pending that decision.
