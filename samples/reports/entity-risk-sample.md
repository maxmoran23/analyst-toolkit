## Domain Findings

### 1. Corporate Profile & Ownership — 55/100

**Observed.** Meridian is presented as a digital-asset exchange operating since 2019, incorporated in an offshore jurisdiction, with a public-facing brand operated through a named local subsidiary in at least two markets. Corporate-registry extracts (synthetic, for this sample) show the operating company held by an intermediate holding company, which is in turn held by a holding vehicle in a second offshore jurisdiction.

**Assessed.** The chain runs at least three layers deep and terminates in a vehicle whose shareholders are not disclosed in any registry reachable from public sources. No named ultimate beneficial owner could be confirmed. Founder-attributed interviews name two individuals as principals, but neither is corroborated as a registered owner in the documents reviewed — that linkage is **alleged, not established**. Ownership opacity of this kind is a recognized money-laundering vulnerability because it frustrates source-of-wealth and control analysis. The score reflects elevated opacity against an otherwise unremarkable corporate age and structure; it is not higher because no affirmatively adverse ownership fact (e.g., a sanctioned or criminally implicated owner) was identified.

### 2. Financial Health — 40/100

**Observed.** Meridian is a private entity and publishes no audited financial statements. Public signals are limited to: a 2021 venture funding round reported by trade press; self-reported trading-volume and user figures on the corporate site and in marketing; and a self-published "proof-of-reserves" attestation page.

**Assessed.** None of these is independently verifiable to a financial-statement standard. Self-reported volume and the ~4M user figure are **claims**, not audited facts. The proof-of-reserves page is a point-in-time attestation, not an audit, and does not address liabilities — a recognized limitation of that format. No going-concern signal (default, missed obligation, insolvency filing) was identified, which is the only reason the score is not higher; equally, no evidence affirmatively supports solvency. The score reflects an unverifiable financial position rather than observed distress. Confidence in this domain is **LOW** and is the principal driver of the information-gap section below.

### 3. Regulatory & Enforcement History — 68/100

**Observed.** Public regulatory records reviewed for this sample show: (a) a **consumer-protection warning** issued by the financial regulator in one jurisdiction, cautioning residents that Meridian is not authorized to offer certain services locally; (b) public statements by regulators in two further markets questioning whether Meridian's local activity falls inside a licensing perimeter; and (c) registration as a money-services / virtual-asset business in one jurisdiction where it does hold a license.

**Assessed.** A consumer-protection warning is a regulatory action and is treated here as an **observed fact**, not an allegation — but it is materially less severe than an enforcement order, consent decree, or penalty, none of which was identified. The unlicensed-perimeter questions in two markets are **open/unresolved** and are treated as pending, not as findings of wrongdoing. The picture is of a firm operating ahead of, or across, licensing perimeters in multiple markets while holding authorization in at least one — a common but elevated posture for a cross-border exchange. The score reflects a confirmed regulator warning plus unresolved perimeter exposure, short of the HIGH band that a formal penalty or license revocation would justify.

### 4. Litigation & Legal Proceedings — 50/100

**Observed.** Court-record and docket searches for this sample surface civil claims brought by users in two jurisdictions alleging inability to withdraw funds and seeking account access and damages. No class action has been certified on the public record reviewed. No criminal indictment, fraud prosecution, or regulatory enforcement litigation naming Meridian or its named principals was identified.

**Assessed.** The civil claims are **pending allegations**, not adjudicated findings, and the prompt's rule is to label them as such. Their significance is less the individual claims than the fact that they corroborate, through the court record, the same withdrawal-access theme that dominates the adverse-media domain — i.e., the complaint is consistent across two independent source types. The absence of any criminal matter holds this domain to the middle of the ELEVATED band rather than higher.

### 5. Sanctions & Watchlist Exposure — 35/100

**Observed.** The entity name, its named operating subsidiaries, and the two individuals publicly described as principals were screened (for this sample) against the OFAC SDN list and the consolidated EU, UN, and UK lists. **No matches were identified.** No principal was identified as a politically exposed person, and no proximity to a sanctioned party was established.

**Assessed.** A clean screen is a genuine and valuable result and is recorded as such. The residual 35 is not a "hit" — it reflects two structural factors the prompt asks the analyst to weigh: (a) UBOs could not be identified (Domain 1), so the screen is necessarily incomplete at the ownership layer — an unscreenable owner is a residual exposure, not a clean result; and (b) the high-risk operating geography (Domain 8) raises the inherent likelihood of sanctioned-nexus customer flow, which a name screen of the entity itself does not capture. The score reflects screening completeness and inherent exposure, not an adverse finding. **The sanctions override does not apply.**

### 6. Adverse Media — 72/100

**Observed.** Adverse-media review for this sample identifies a **sustained, multi-source pattern** of negative coverage concentrated on customer withdrawal delays, degraded support responsiveness, and disputed account access, reported by named trade outlets and consumer-affairs press across more than 18 months. Coverage is recurring rather than a single episode, and it intensifies around the period of the regulator warning in Domain 3.

**Assessed.** Adverse media is, by nature, **allegation and reportage** — it is not treated as proof of wrongdoing. Its weight here comes from three properties the prompt directs the analyst to assess: persistence (sustained over 18+ months, not a one-off), multi-source corroboration (independent outlets), and cross-domain corroboration (the same theme appears in the court record, Domain 4, and aligns with the regulator warning, Domain 3). A pattern that is durable, independently reported, and consistent with the regulatory and litigation record is a stronger signal than isolated negative press. No investigative report alleging fraud, theft of customer assets, or criminal conduct was identified — which is the ceiling on this score. It sits at the top of the ELEVATED band.

### 7. Governance & Integrity — 62/100

**Observed.** Meridian discloses limited governance information publicly. The corporate site names an executive team but provides minimal board disclosure. Two different external accounting/attestation firms are associated with Meridian's published attestations across the review period, indicating a **change of external provider**.

**Assessed.** External auditor/attestor turnover is a recognized governance flag because it can — though does not necessarily — signal a disagreement over scope, findings, or fees; on the public record reviewed, **no reason for the change is disclosed**, so the change is noted as a flag, not interpreted as evidence of a dispute. Thin board disclosure limits any assessment of independent oversight. No principal was identified as having held a senior role in a failed or sanctioned entity. The score reflects auditor turnover plus weak governance transparency, partially offset by the absence of any affirmatively adverse integrity finding against a named individual.

The sector — centralized digital-asset exchange offering custody, staking, and hosted wallets to retail users cross-border — is inherently high money-laundering risk.

**Assessed.** This is the highest domain score, and it is driven by **inherent** risk — the jurisdiction-and-sector combination — rather than by any specific adverse event. A retail-facing crypto exchange custodying assets from a high-risk base carries structurally elevated exposure to layering, mule activity, and sanctions-evasion typologies regardless of conduct. The score reflects that inherent profile. ## Red Flags

2. **Regulator consumer-protection warning.** A financial regulator in one jurisdiction has publicly warned residents that Meridian is not authorized to offer certain services there (Domain 3) — an observed regulatory action, not an allegation.
3. **Sustained adverse media on withdrawal delays.** An 18+ month, multi-source pattern of negative coverage on customer withdrawal access and support failures (Domain 6), independently corroborated by the civil court record (Domain 4).
4. **High-risk primary operating jurisdiction.** Incorporation and core operations sit in a jurisdiction in an elevated-risk band on recognized country-risk indices, compounding the inherent risk of an already high-risk sector (Domain 8).
5. **External auditor / attestor turnover.** A change of external attestation provider with no publicly disclosed reason (Domain 7) — a governance flag warranting a direct question to the counterparty.

## Recommended Disposition

**Proceed with conditions — escalate for senior review.**

Meridian is not declinable on the present public record: no sanctions hit, no criminal matter, no formal enforcement penalty, and no domain in the SEVERE band. But an ELEVATED composite of 57 — driven by ownership opacity, a regulator warning, a sustained adverse-media pattern, and a high-risk geography — exceeds what a standard onboarding can absorb. Escalate to a senior approver and condition any onboarding on the following, each of which must be satisfied and documented before the relationship goes live:

1. **Full UBO disclosure.** A complete ownership chart to ultimate natural-person owners, with supporting registry/legal evidence, and adverse-media plus sanctions screening of every disclosed UBO. Onboarding does not proceed while any UBO is unscreenable.
2. **Financial transparency.** Audited financial statements, or an independent solvency opinion, for the two most recent periods. A proof-of-reserves attestation alone does not satisfy this condition.
3. **Licensing position.** Written confirmation of Meridian's authorization status in each market relevant to the proposed relationship, including its position on the two unresolved perimeters.
4. **Withdrawal-integrity explanation.** A documented response to the withdrawal-delay pattern evidenced in the adverse-media and litigation record, including current processing-time metrics and customer-asset segregation arrangements.
5. **Auditor-change explanation.** A direct, documented explanation for the external attestor turnover.
6. **Enhanced ongoing monitoring.** If onboarded, classify the relationship high-risk: enhanced transaction monitoring, an accelerated periodic-review cycle, and a defined adverse-media re-screen cadence.

**Escalation / re-decline triggers.** Reassess immediately and move toward decline if, before or after onboarding: a sanctions nexus emerges for the entity or any UBO; a formal enforcement action, consent order, or penalty is issued by any regulator; a criminal matter is initiated against the entity or a principal; or the counterparty cannot or will not satisfy conditions 1–3.
