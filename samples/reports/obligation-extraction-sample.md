## Scope & Applicability

FATF Recommendation 16 ("R.16") sets a standard for the information that must accompany electronic value transfers so that originator and beneficiary information travels with the transfer and is available to authorities and to the institutions in the payment chain. FATF has confirmed that R.16 applies to transfers of **virtual assets**, and the requirement as applied to virtual asset transfers is widely referred to as the **"Travel Rule."**

Read from the perspective of a **VASP** that conducts virtual asset transfers, the standard governs the VASP both as an **originating institution** (when it sends a transfer on a customer's instruction) and as a **beneficiary institution** (when it receives a transfer for a customer), and addresses **intermediary** institutions in a transfer chain. The core in-scope activity is the transfer of a virtual asset from one person to another where a VASP is involved on one or both sides.

Two threshold and carve-out points are central and are themselves a recurring source of interpretive difficulty (see Ambiguities):

- **A de minimis threshold.** FATF's standard contemplates a de minimis threshold (commonly referenced at the **USD/EUR 1,000** level) below which a reduced set of information may accompany a transfer. The existence and exact level of any threshold in practice depend on the transposing jurisdiction.
- **Non-custodial / unhosted wallets.** Transfers to or from wallets not controlled by an obliged VASP ("unhosted" or self-hosted wallets) are treated differently from VASP-to-VASP transfers; the obligations a VASP can discharge depend on whether a counterparty institution exists.

A reader should note before using the register: FATF sets the standard, but a VASP's *actual, enforceable* obligations — including the threshold, the unhosted-wallet treatment, and the consequences of non-compliance — are established by the law of the jurisdiction(s) in which it is regulated, not by FATF directly.

## Key Deadlines

- **At the time the transfer is made** — Obligations 3, 4, 9, 11 (transmit required information immediately and securely; intermediaries pass information on; sanctions/freezing action applies immediately).
- **Ongoing / procedures in place** — Obligations 7, 8 (beneficiary-side detection of missing information; risk-based execute/reject/suspend procedures), and 12 (risk-based procedure for un-obtainable information).
- **Tied to CDD timing** — Obligation 5 (verification of originator information), per the transposing regime.
- **Recurring / retention** — Obligation 10 (record retention; commonly referenced as at least five years under FATF R.11).
- **No deadline stated in the FATF standard** — Obligations 1, 2, 6 record information-content and conditional requirements that are not themselves time-bound in the FATF text; their timing is set by the transposing jurisdiction's rule and its compliance date.

Note: the FATF standard does not itself carry a single "effective date." The operative compliance date for any VASP is the effective date of the **transposing law** in its jurisdiction — which this register cannot supply and which must be confirmed against that jurisdiction's rule.

## Ambiguities & Open Questions

- **The de minimis threshold — existence and level (Obligation 6).** The FATF standard contemplates a de minimis threshold and a commonly referenced USD/EUR 1,000 figure, but whether a threshold applies, and at what level, is left to the transposing jurisdiction. Reasonable readers in different jurisdictions will reach different conclusions on whether a sub-threshold transfer may carry reduced information. *Open question: what threshold, if any, applies under the specific transposing law the VASP is subject to?*
- **Unhosted / non-custodial wallets (Obligation 12).** When a transfer involves a wallet not controlled by an obliged institution, there is no counterparty VASP to send information to or receive it from. The standard contemplates risk-based treatment, but exactly **what** a VASP must collect or do for an unhosted-wallet transfer — and whether any collection obligation attaches at all — is interpretation-dependent and varies sharply by jurisdiction. *Open question: what, if anything, must a VASP collect or verify for transfers to/from unhosted wallets under the applicable law?*
- **Counterparty VASP identification and due diligence.** R.16 assumes the originating VASP can identify the beneficiary institution to transmit information to it. The standard does not, in the text parsed here, prescribe a single method for **identifying and vetting** the counterparty VASP before transmitting customer information to it. *Open question: what counterparty-VASP identification and due-diligence steps are required before transmitting personal information?*
- **"Immediately" (Obligations 3, 4).** The standard requires information to accompany the transfer and be transmitted "immediately." Whether "immediately" permits transmission shortly before, exactly at, or shortly after the on-chain transfer settles is not defined with precision in the text parsed here. *Open question: what transmission timing, relative to settlement, satisfies "immediately"?*
- **Interaction of the sunrise problem with Obligation 8.** Where jurisdictions have transposed the Travel Rule on different timelines ("sunrise issue"), a compliant VASP may transact with a counterparty institution not yet subject to the rule. How the risk-based execute/reject/suspend procedure (Obligation 8) should treat that situation is a practical interpretation question the standard does not resolve. *Open question: how should the missing-information procedure treat a counterparty in a jurisdiction that has not yet implemented the rule?*

These items are listed as open questions, not as settled obligations. They should be routed to counsel and resolved against the transposing jurisdiction's enacted rule and any local supervisory guidance.
