# Methodology — Fraud-Detection Transaction and Session Triage

This document is the regulator-facing specification for [`scorer.py`](scorer.py).
The evidence in [`evidence/`](evidence/) is generated by
[`run_validation.py`](run_validation.py); numbers are not authored into the report.

> **In plain terms:** Fraud decisions have two serious error costs. Approving known
> fraud creates loss; hard-declining legitimate activity creates customer and
> revenue harm. This engine therefore has two build gates, not the one-sided recall
> gate appropriate to many screening systems. Both sides must have zero observed
> failures and enough labelled examples to put the exact 95% upper failure-rate
> bound at or below 0.1%.

## 1. Intended use and decision boundary

The unit of analysis is one transaction/session event against one customer
baseline. The output is a routing recommendation. The engine does not execute a
decline, block, filing, freeze, reimbursement decision, or customer communication.
Human operators and governed downstream systems own every action.

The model is deterministic, has no learned weights, and maintains no hidden state.
Its inputs depend on upstream identity, device, authentication, merchant,
beneficiary, credit, and transaction-aggregation controls; those sources require
separate validation.

## 2. Inputs

`CustomerBaseline` supplies expected event amount, expected ten-minute event count,
relationship tenure, and customer ID. `FraudEvent` supplies session, CNP, merchant,
credit, inflow/flow-through, account-age, identity, beneficiary, device, and
authentication signals. Amount and velocity are expressed as ratios to the
customer baseline with `_lib.aggregations.ratio_to_expected`.

## 3. Named typology rules

Every rule is implemented through `_lib.rules.RuleSet` and yields its name,
typology, severity, detail, and corroborating causes.

| Rule | Fires only when all conditions hold | Severity | Named corroboration |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| `account_takeover` | new device; at least 3 failed logins; contact change within 24 hours | `min(1.00, 0.88 + 0.02 * (failures - 3))` | `new_device`, `credential_failures`, `recent_contact_change` |
| `card_not_present` | CNP; cross-border; amount at least 4.0x expected; merchant trust at most 0.25 | `min(0.87, 0.76 + 0.015 * (ratio - 4))` | `card_not_present`, `cross_border_merchant`, `amount_profile_break`, `low_trust_merchant` |
| `first_party_bustout` | utilization at least 0.95; at least 2 returned payments in 90 days; amount at least 2.0x expected | `min(0.87, 0.81 + 0.02 * (returns - 2))` | `near_limit_utilization`, `repeated_returned_payments`, `spend_profile_break` |
| `mule_inflow` | at least 5 inbound senders in 72 hours; same-day outflow at least 90% of inflow; account no older than 180 days | `min(1.00, 0.90 + 0.01 * (senders - 5))` | `unrelated_sender_fanin`, `same_day_flowthrough`, `young_account` |
| `synthetic_identity` | at least 3 conflicting identity attributes; reused identity document; account no older than 90 days | `min(1.00, 0.94 + 0.01 * (conflicts - 3))` | `identity_attribute_conflicts`, `reused_identity_document`, `new_identity_relationship` |

Each threshold is inclusive. The generator plants exact-threshold positive cases to
test boundary behavior, plus legitimate mimics that carry one or two surface
signals but do not satisfy a complete hard rule.

## 4. Score and disposition contract

The continuous score supports ordering and calibration only:

```text
deviation = saturating(max(0, amount_ratio - 1)
                       + max(0, velocity_ratio - 1), scale=4)
score = clamp(max(max_fired_rule_severity, deviation), 0, 1)
```

Disposition is independent of any bare score threshold:

1. If a named typology fired with at least two named corroborating causes, set a
   hard risk floor. Severity at least `0.88` maps to
   `REFER_FOR_BLOCK_CONFIRMATION`; lower severity maps to
   `DECLINE_PENDING_REVIEW`.
2. If a typology somehow fires without two causes, route to `STEP_UP_AUTH`. Current
   rules structurally require at least three causes, but the branch makes the
   corroboration contract explicit.
3. With no fired typology, `APPROVE` is permitted only when all
   `trusted_session_continuity` facts hold: known device, strong authentication, no
   new device, zero failed logins, no recent contact change, known beneficiary,
   beneficiary risk below 0.50, zero identity conflicts, and no reused document.
   Amount and ten-minute velocity must each also be no more than 1.5x baseline.
4. Every other no-rule event maps to `STEP_UP_AUTH` under the named cause
   `behavioral_deviation_without_corroboration`.

The rank order is `APPROVE < STEP_UP_AUTH < DECLINE_PENDING_REVIEW <
REFER_FOR_BLOCK_CONFIRMATION`. `_max_disposition` applies the typology floor after
all scoring. No mitigant is applied below that point, so risk floors are raise-only.

## 5. Dual safety invariant

The invariant is symmetric and enforced independently on both labelled classes:

```text
fraud_miss = confirmed fraud receiving APPROVE
false_decline = legitimate activity receiving DECLINE_PENDING_REVIEW
                or REFER_FOR_BLOCK_CONFIRMATION

for each metric:
    observed failure count == 0
    exact one-sided 95% Clopper-Pearson upper failure-rate bound <= 0.001
```

`STEP_UP_AUTH` is deliberately excluded from false decline because it requests
additional authentication without making a hard adverse recommendation. With zero
failures, the fraud side needs at least 2,995 labelled fraud events to satisfy the
statistical gate; the default 50,000-event population contains exactly 4,000. The
legitimate side contains 46,000. The bound is computed by direct binomial inversion
in the vendored `_lib.sampling`; no normal approximation or external package is
used.

Every additional trial is gated independently. Failure injection changes one
harness record after scoring, prints its event ID, returns a nonzero exit status,
and never writes evidence. This makes both branches directly testable without
weakening production logic.

## 6. Synthetic population and metrics

The generator creates exactly 8% confirmed fraud, distributes it across all five
typologies, and labels every event with its ground truth, typology, and category.
Each typology receives inclusive boundary cases. Legitimate mimics cover new-device
travel, a high-value known payee, recurring CNP bursts, young-account payroll,
credit rebuilding, and identity refresh. Trusted continuity is separately labelled.

The harness reports fraud recall, legitimate false-decline rate, exact bounds,
intervention precision and lift over prevalence, the disposition funnel,
per-typology recall, per-legitimate-category outcomes, a score-only counterfactual
sweep, and multi-seed stability. Throughput is volatile telemetry and therefore
appears only on stdout, never in deterministic evidence.

## 7. Model-risk governance

The implementation follows SR 11-7-style principles relevant to a transparent rule
engine: documented purpose and limitations, independent challenge, outcome
analysis, change control, reproducible evidence, and ongoing monitoring. A real
owner must validate input lineage, sampling representativeness, segment outcomes,
policy/legal treatment of step-up and declines, drift, override behavior, and
downstream case handling. Synthetic results cannot establish live performance.

**Confidence rating: HIGH —** every stated rule and gate maps directly to executable
code and generated evidence; confidence in live-data generalization is LOW until
institution-specific validation is completed.
